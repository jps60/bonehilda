ITEM-STAND DUSTING: SIMPLE, VARIED AND SHAPE-AWARE

The user wants 3-5 distinct, reusable dusting variations for stands at a few
heights. Select/adapt the motion using the outer limits of the DISPLAYED ITEM's
model, not merely the stand's wooden support or mounting board. A mounted sword
should guide the contact path; it is not enough to brush an unrelated stand edge.

Interpret this as 3-5 small motion families adaptable across orientation and
height, rather than a unique clip for every item or dozens of near-identical
clips. Four families are a reasonable initial target: lengthwise sweeps for
elongated shapes, broad arcs for wide faces, short contour strokes for irregular
shapes, and small detail/flick strokes. Choose better families if the geometry
suggests them. Report the actual baked clip count and adaptation scheme.

Cover horizontal and vertical presentation, with low/tabletop, chest-level and
just-overhead contact bands relative to the actor's body. Explicitly demonstrate
both chest-height and just-overhead wall mounts. Avoid fixed world heights that
fit only the zero-star maid. Prefer parameterized contact targets/IK or reusable
offsets where feasible; keep individual motions simple and readable.

Use projected visible-model bounds or a conservative silhouette/coarse hull to
choose a style and outer stroke limits. Include attachment rotation and scale.
Bounds alone are not evidence of a safe contact path: check exposed surfaces and
avoid routing the hand/shaft through the bounding-box center of a hollow pot,
shield, sword guard, trophy, or armor display. Exclude mounting walls, hidden
LODs, helper geometry and unrelated VFX from the displayed item's shape bounds.

Show representative long/narrow, broad/flat, irregular and bulky objects (for
example a sword, shield, trophy and helmet/armor) on both stand orientations.
Vary their sizes and rotations. Provide a safe empty-stand case or mark it as a
skip. For armor stands, use equipped visible bounds/pose, not just the bare stand.

The goal is reduced obvious clipping, not expensive exact collision simulation.
Slight feather compression/overlap is acceptable. The handle, hand, forearm and
broom shaft should not sweep deeply through solid objects, the wall, her skull,
the apron, or her legs. Prefer backing off, shortening a stroke, changing the
stance, selecting another variation or skipping an unsafe target. Do not stretch
the arm or produce exaggerated jumps to reach an unsuitable mount.
